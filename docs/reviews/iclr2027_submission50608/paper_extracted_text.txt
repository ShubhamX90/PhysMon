ABSTRACT
A language model’s conﬁdence on a physics problem deserves trust only if it
ignores details that play no part in the physics, yet models often shift their
conﬁdence when such a detail changes, for instance the temperature of a wall
or the torque of a mechanically isolated second system. Unmeasured, this sen-
sitivity undermines any use of conﬁdence as a reliability signal. Measuring it
has been hard: perturbed benchmarks cannot certify that an edit leaves the an-
swer unchanged, and it is expensive to observe, since the effect surfaces only
across several queries. We introduce DECOY1 (Distractor-Embedded Certiﬁed
cOunterfactuals for ph Ysics): 155 families of four physics prompts that differ in
a single irrelevant detail. A symbolic solver certiﬁes for every family that the de-
tail cannot affect the answer, and each family is labelled by the largest drop in
the model’s log-probability of the certiﬁed answer, at a threshold ﬁxed before
any probe was trained. Across two open-weight 7–8B instruction-tuned models,
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is sensitive on 50.0% of the 140 of 155 families that yield
a label and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 22.9% of the same families. The largest ef-
fects occur when a distractor lies numerically close to the answer. A linear probe
on Qwen’s layer-18 hidden state, averaged over a family’s four renderings, pre-
dicts this sensitivity before any token is generated at AUROC 0.760 (a ranking
score from 0.5, chance, to 1.0, perfect) with whole families held out. Scored on a
single rendering instead, a separately tuned probe reaches 0.747. A layer-16 probe
frozen after training reaches 0.820 pooled AUROC on the 15 new families built af-
terwards, though within-cue-type AUROC is lower (0.667–0.6875, Section 5.2).
Under a paired bootstrap over families the probe performs on par with a ﬁtted
token-entropy baseline (0.727) and reaches the lowest Brier score (a calibration
measure, lower is better) of any predictor we test (0.204 against 0.214). Knock-
ing out four attention heads at layer 16 cancels the sensitivity, localising it to a
narrow, targetable mechanism. In Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, a conﬁdent answer can
therefore silently track task-irrelevant detail, and the failure is detectable before
generation and traced to a location a defender can act on. DECOY supplies the
certiﬁed ground truth and the ﬁtted, paired-tested baselines against which future
hidden-state monitors should be judged.
1 I NTRODUCTION
Consider the problem “Object A has mass 2.0 kg and moves at 6.0 m/s. The laboratory ﬂoor area
is 10 m 2. What is the kinetic energy of object A?” The ﬂoor area appears in no equation a physicist
would write, and a symbolic check of the governing expression conﬁrms it. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct
answers 36.0 J, correctly, on this prompt and on three copies in which the ﬂoor area is 20, 40 and 80
m2. Its conﬁdence moves all the same. The log-probability it assigns to the correct answer falls by
1.03 nats between the ﬁrst and the last copy. A nat is the unit of natural-log probability, and a drop
of 0.5 nats means the probability falls by a factor of about 1.65. Across the benchmark introduced
here, this model shows a drop of at least 0.5 nats on half of all labelled problem families.

A model whose conﬁdence depends on details that the physics says to ignore is a reliability concern,
and the concern is sharpest when such dependence can be found and ﬂagged before an answer
is used. Two things stand in the way. The ﬁrst is measurement. Benchmarks built by perturbing
existing problems (Shi et al., 2023; Mirzadeh et al., 2025) rely on template or heuristic construction
rather than a formal proof that a perturbation preserves the correct answer. As a result, a change in
output can still mix instability in the model with an unveriﬁed change in the task. The second is cost.
Sensitivity is a property of a set of prompts, and observing it takes several queries per problem. We
address both. DECOY contains 155 families, each made of four renderings of one physics problem
that differ only in a designated cue, a detail that plays no role in the answer. For every family a
computer algebra system ( Meurer et al. , 2017) proves that the cue symbol is absent from the solved
expression for the answer, so any change in the model’s behaviour across the family belongs to the
model. The certiﬁcate also gives the measurement a clear reference point. A model that answers by
identifying the governing law and the quantities it depends on computes the same thing on all four
prompts, so its probability for the certiﬁed answer is the same on all four. The size of the largest drop
across the family therefore measures how far the cue reaches into the model’s computation, and it
is zero for an ideal solver. We then ask whether the model’s own hidden state on a single prompt
already predicts that drop, which would let a deployed system ﬂag a risky question with one forward
pass and before any answer is produced.
It does. A linear probe on the hidden state (the residual stream) at layer 18 of Qwen’s 28 layers, at the
last prompt token, predicts the family’s label, averaged over a family’s four renderings, at AUROC
0.760 with whole families held out. Scored on a single rendering instead, a separately tuned probe
reaches 0.747. It exceeds a classiﬁer on the prompt’s words by 0.11 and holds at 0.738 after the part
explained by a correctness probe is removed. A layer-16 probe with frozen weights reaches 0.820
pooled on ﬁfteen families built after training, though within-cue-type AUROC is lower (Section 5.2).
A monitor earns its place by adding to what cheaper signals already provide, so we compare it with
eight non-activation predictors on the same families under a paired family bootstrap. In this boot-
strap, both predictors are scored on identical resamples and the interval is taken on their difference.
The probe outperforms every text-based and behavioural predictor, from a chain-of-thought classiﬁer
(AUROC 0.599) to an LLM judge (AUROC 0.480), and performs on par with a ﬁtted token-entropy
baseline (AUROC 0.727), a classiﬁer trained on the model’s output uncertainty. It also reaches the
lowest Brier score of any single predictor. This comparison is stricter than the random-direction
and surface baselines common in probing work, and the closeness of the entropy baseline is itself a
useful result. It tells practitioners which cheap signal to deploy alongside a probe. Finally, knocking
out four attention heads at layer 16 of Qwen cancels the sensitivity on a 20-family panel. The mean
normalised recovery is 1.870 (recovery above 1.0 reﬂects an over-reversal of the drop, and capping
each family at 1.0 gives 0.969), against 0.021 for random perturbations of the same size.
Contributions. (a) A certiﬁed benchmark: DECOY, 155 solver-certiﬁed counterfactual physics
families over three cue types and three domains, with stored certiﬁcates and a three-level framework
that keeps prompt condition, behaviour and internal state apart (Section 3). (b) A measurement of
sensitivity: under a pre-registered threshold, sensitivity is common, differs sharply between models,
and peaks when a distractor nearly matches the answer (Section 5.1). (c) A pre-generation monitor:
prompt-side hidden states predict sensitivity with families held out, and a frozen probe retains signal
on new families built afterwards (Section 5.2). (d) An evaluation standard: a paired comparison
against ﬁtted baselines that any future hidden-state monitor can be checked against (Section 5.3). (e)
A causal handle in Qwen: a localised set of attention heads whose removal cancels the behaviour
(Section 5.4).

design and add a symbolic proof that each edit preserves the answer , which makes the measured
change a property of the model. In mathematical reasoning, irrelevant context lowers accuracy on

Faithfulness and monitoring. A model’s written reasoning does not always reﬂect the features that
drive its answer ( Turpin et al. , 2023; Lanham et al. , 2023). The same holds for reasoning-trained
models ( Chen et al. , 2025) and for reasoning produced without adversarial prompting ( Arcuschin
et al., 2026). Monitoring the chain of thought is a valuable safety opportunity that is also imperfect
and fragile ( Korbak et al. , 2025), which motivates complementary monitors that read the model’s
internal state. We test that motivation directly: chain-of-thought and answer-rationale classiﬁers
run as baselines against a hidden-state probe on the same families, on a label whose correctness is
certiﬁed rather than assumed.
Probing internal states. Linear probes on intermediate representations ( Alain & Bengio , 2017)
are a standard tool, and interpreting them requires separating what the probe learns from what the
model encodes ( Belinkov, 2022; Hewitt & Liang , 2019). We adapt the control-task logic of Hewitt
& Liang (2019) to this setting. Our domain-generalisation check tests the probe’s signal against
label-shufﬂed permutations (Appendix H), and we separately ask whether a non-activation predic-
tor already reaches the same held-out score. We also score a generic correctness probe as a full
comparator, since a problem that makes a model unstable is often a hard problem.
Truthfulness, self-knowledge and uncertainty. A closely related line reads truthfulness and cor-
rectness from internal states. Contrast-consistent search recovers a truth direction without labels
(Burns et al. , 2023). Supervised probes detect when a model is producing a false statement ( Azaria
& Mitchell, 2023), and true and false statements separate along linear directions ( Marks & Tegmark,
2024). We instead read a direction for sensitivity to a detail whose irrelevance is proven, not as-
sumed. Models can also estimate the probability that their own answers are correct ( Kadavath et al.,
2022), and representation engineering reads and steers such concepts at scale ( Zou et al. , 2023).
On the output side, semantic entropy detects hallucination from the distribution of sampled answers
(Farquhar et al. , 2024). Semantic entropy probes instead recover a sampling-based estimate from a
single generation ( Kossen et al. , 2024), INSIDE reads cross-generation hidden-state covariance to
detect hallucination ( Chen et al. , 2024), and perturbation-based representational stability calibrates
conﬁdence directly (Khanmohammadi et al., 2025). These target correctness and hallucination under
free generation, and evaluating them on DECOY is a natural extension of the comparator ladder in
Section 5.3. We place a hidden-state probe, a correctness probe and ﬁtted output-uncertainty base-
lines side by side on the same families , which shows how much of what a probe reads is shared with
signals that need no access to activations.
Causal localisation. Activation patching and causal mediation analysis identify components whose
alteration changes behaviour ( Meng et al. , 2022; Vig et al. , 2020; Wang et al., 2023; Conmy et al. ,
2023), with known interpretive pitfalls ( Heimersheim & Nanda , 2024), and competing mechanisms

3 M ETHODOLOGY
3.1 T HREE -LEVEL FRAMEWORK
We separate three levels of description (Figure 1). Level 1 is the prompt condition: which value of
the cue a prompt contains. Level 2 is observed behaviour : how much the model’s output moves
across a family whose correct answer is certiﬁed constant. Level 3 is the internal state : a prompt-
side hidden state that predicts Level 2, or a component whose removal changes it. Our claims follow
the chain Level 3 → predicts → Level 2 → correlated with → Level 1. Level 2 is a statement about
the model’s output. Whether the model consulted the cue as a reasoning step is a further question,
and we keep the two apart.

Cue types. Cue A adds a physically irrelevant variable, such as a wall temperature in a centripetal-
force problem. Cue B adds a distractor with the same units as the answer that the stated setup makes
irrelevant, such as the torque of a second, mechanically isolated system. Units alone cannot rule it
out, so Cue B is the most diagnostic type. Cue C states the same quantity in equivalent units (200
W, 0.2 kW, 200 J/s, 200000 mW) and is admitted only when the conversion is exact. Families are
generated template-ﬁrst. The symbolic problem and its certiﬁcate exist before any text. Language
models may paraphrase and vary variable names. They never write physics or answers (AppendixC).

5.2 H IDDEN STATES PREDICT SENSITIVITY BEFORE GENERATION
The layer-18 probe, averaged over a family’s four renderings, reaches AUROC 0.760 (95% inter-
val [0.675, 0.838]) with families held out (Figure 2, right). Scored on a single rendering instead, a
separately swept probe reaches 0.747 at layer 16 (Appendix E). The peak sits at 64% of network
depth in Qwen and at 69% in DeepSeek. The signal comes from processing inside the model. A
TF–IDF classiﬁer on the prompt reaches 0.651 and a probe on the input embeddings 0.567. It is
stable across labelling choices, with AUROC between 0.722 and 0.782 as the threshold sweeps from
0.1 to 2.0 nats, and needs only part of the family. A separate ablation, on a smaller Stage 6 panel
distinct from the primary 135-family sweep, gives 0.735 averaging two renderings against 0.731
with all four. Sensitive families are often hard ones (the correctness probe’s score correlates with the
label at −0.346), and after removing the part of the score that correctness explains, the probe keeps
0.738 (95% interval [0.650, 0.819]). The effect replicates in DeepSeek, where a probe on the spread
of hidden states across renderings reaches 0.703 against that model’s own labels. A layer-16 probe
frozen after training scores 0.820 (AUPRC 0.907) on ﬁfteen families built afterwards, split 5 Cue
B and 10 Cue C. The pooled ﬁgure overstates within-type discrimination, since Cue C predictions
cluster high and Cue B cluster low. AUROC is 0.667 on Cue B alone and 0.6875 on Cue C alone,
each too small to resolve (Section 7).
5.3 C OMPARISON WITH NON -ACTIVATION BASELINES
The probe exceeds every behavioural and text-based predictor (Figure 3a), from the black-box two-
query check (0.668) through the chain-of-thought classiﬁer (0.599) to the zero-shot LLM judge
(0.480). Two predictors come close. The ﬁtted entropy baseline reaches 0.727, and the paired dif-
ference of +0.033 has a 95% interval of [−0.074, +0.141] (Figure 3b), so on 135 families the two
are statistically indistinguishable. Descriptively, the probe ranks higher in 72% of resamples. The
correctness probe reaches 0.766, consistent with the overlap between sensitivity and difﬁculty noted
above. Adding hidden states to the combined non-activation model raises its AUROC from 0.705 to
0.772, a gain of +0.067 whose interval [−0.001, +0.137] includes zero. The probe also reaches the
lowest Brier score of any single predictor, 0.204 against 0.214 for entropy and 0.229 for the com-

5.4 C AUSAL INTERVENTION
Knocking out heads 26, 24, 13 and 11 at layer 16 of Qwen gives a mean normalised recovery of
1.870 (95% interval [1.560, 2.198]) on the 20-family Cue B panel (Figure 4). A recovery above 1
means the intervention inverts the drop, so the model becomes more conﬁdent under the distractor
rendering than under its own base rendering. Capping each family’s recovery at 1 gives 0.969. Head
11 carries almost all of the effect alone (1.848, leaving 0.022 for the other three heads combined),
and the window is one layer wide. The same head gives 0.540 at layer 15 and −0.049 at layer 17
(Appendix F). The controls separate this effect from generic disruption: twenty equal-norm random
perturbations give 0.021 and are exceeded on 20 of 20 families, the random-site control reaches
0.374, and the unrelated-family donor gives −0.037. Accuracy on 40 held-out families moves from
0.700 to 0.713, inside a 5% tolerance ﬁxed in advance, showing the intervention does not simply
damage the model. The stable cue-bearing donor recovers 0.590, 32% of head 11’s own 1.848,
which attributes 68% (0.681) of the effect speciﬁcally to sensitivity. The same-answer donor, a
complementary check on whether the effect tracks answer identity, recovers even less (0.405). The
same heads give 2.177 on ten held-out Cue A and Cue C families (capped mean 0.702), a result
carried almost entirely by Cue A. Four of the ﬁve Cue C families recover below 0.35 (Appendix F).
The components the intervention touches transfer reliably to Cue A, while the directions a linear
probe reads are speciﬁc to each cue type (cosine 0.018 between Cue A and Cue B). Adding the probe
direction to stable families raises their Slp θ steadily, but only 37% cross the sensitivity threshold at twice the probe’s own scale. The heads, in other words, are necessary for the behaviour. The probe
direction alone is only partly sufﬁcient for it. In Llama and DeepSeek the same recipe gives no
reliable effect at the sites tested (Appendix F).
5.5 D ISCUSSION
Certiﬁcation makes sensitivity a property of the model. Because every family carries a proof that
its cue cannot change the answer, a conﬁdence shift on DECOY is attributable to the model. The
numbers then separate by model (Section 5.1), with 50% sensitive families for Qwen and 23% for
Llama, only 25 shared, and a probe for the cue value itself scoring 0.532, so the label carries no
surface signature of the edit. The largest effects come from numerical proximity, a distractor that
nearly matches the answer (Section 5.1). The cue types also differ in how Qwen and DeepSeek
respond to them (Appendix G), so a single robustness score would hide structure that the certiﬁed,
per-cue view exposes.
What the hidden state carries. Since the probe performs on par with the ﬁtted entropy baseline at
this sample size (Section 5.3), the layer-18 state at the last prompt token already holds much of what

the model’s next-token distribution will later express. The panel is not powered to conﬁrm this pre-

baseline reads only the output-token logits from the same forward pass. The probe also reaches

A localised mechanism with cue-speciﬁc readouts. The layer-16 heads (Section 5.4) generalise
to Cue A but not reliably to Cue C, while the probe directions for Cue A and Cue B are nearly
orthogonal. The picture is one of components shared between Cue A and Cue B that route the dis-
tractor’s inﬂuence upstream, with cue-speciﬁc directions handling the downstream readout. The two
sufﬁciency tests (Appendix H) sharpen it, showing the heads are necessary for the behaviour while
the probe direction alone accounts for only part of it. The behaviour is therefore carried by more of
the activation state than one linear direction. The depth at which the probe peaks (Section 5.2) does
not by itself locate a causal circuit, since the DeepSeek knockout at the matching depth recovers
only a small fraction of the effect (Appendix F).
Where the monitor fails, and what to build next. The probe’s most conﬁdent errors are families
that instantiate the template of a sensitive problem without a near-matching number, and its largest
misses are strong positives whose signature is spread beyond the layer-18 mean feature (AppendixI).
The probe therefore reads template class well and numerical proximity poorly, which points to a
numerically aware, per-rendering representation as the next monitor. The ﬂag it produces should be
trusted to the extent that it beats a ﬁtted uncertainty baseline under a paired test.
6 C ONCLUSION
DECOY makes sensitivity to irrelevant physics details a certiﬁed quantity: every family carries a
proof that its cue cannot change the answer, so a change in the model’s conﬁdence is attributable
to the model. On this benchmark, sensitivity is common, depends strongly on the model, peaks at
numerical near-matches, and is predictable from the model’s own hidden state before it generates
a token, with a causal handle localised, in Qwen, to four attention heads at layer 16. For a prac-
titioner building on this result, the ﬁnding to carry forward is not the headline AUROC but the
evaluation standard behind it. A hidden-state monitor should be judged against the cheapest base-
line that could plausibly explain its accuracy, scored on the same resamples, not against a surface or
random-direction baseline that ﬂatters it.
7 L IMITATIONS
Sample size. The monitoring panel has 135 families with 70 positives. This resolves the probe’s
margins of 0.11 to 0.28 over surface, text and judge baselines, and is too small to resolve differences
of 0.03 to 0.05 against ﬁtted entropy. A larger panel would settle that comparison. The reported
layer-18 AUROC is the best of a 28-layer sweep, and held-out intervals are not adjusted for that
selection.
Scope of the causal result. The head-knockout effect is established in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Nor-
malised recovery divides by the original drop and becomes unstable on families with small drops. A
panel restricted to large drops in each model is needed to assess Llama and DeepSeek.
Coverage. Cue C has ﬁve labelled families, so its large effects are illustrative, and the same small-
sample caveat limits the frozen-probe transfer check (Section 5.2). Held-out families share a tem-
plate cluster with training families in 71% of cases (Appendix K), so leave-one-family-out is not
leave-one-cluster-out. Certiﬁcation is symbolic and complete. A planned human validation will
check whether each cue also reads as irrelevant to a physicist. Holding out whole domains gives
AUROC 0.750, statistically indistinguishable from a permutation-shufﬂed null ( p = 0 .368) at this
sample size, so generalisation to unseen domains is not established (full scope in Appendix J).

symbolic templates. It involves no human-subjects data, no personal data and no scraped web con-

C G ENERATION PROTOCOL
Eight steps. Section 3.2 states the principle. Every family is generated under the same eight-step
protocol. (a) Deﬁne a canonical symbolic problem: the relevant variables r, assumptions a, govern-
ing principle g, and the solver-derived answer y∗. (b) Designate the cue z and write the symbolic
proof or executable certiﬁcate that y∗ is invariant to it. (c) Instantiate numerical settings for r, vary-
ing z across four values per family. (d) Render matched natural-language prompts such that only the
recorded cue span changes across family members. (e) Generate paraphrased and variable-renamed
variants for robustness testing. (f) Run automatic checks for dimensional consistency, answer invari-
ance through the parser, assumption completeness, and surface-statistic matching on length, number
count, variable count and unit-token count. (g) Run adversarial artefact analysis, prompting a sepa-
rate model to predict the sensitivity class from the prompt alone and inspecting the features it uses.
(h) Specify dual human validation as the ﬁnal protocol step. The pilot has not yet been run for the
families in this release (Appendix K). Language models are permitted to paraphrase, diversify vari-
able names, discover adversarial artefacts, and propose candidate failure cases for human review.
They are prohibited from inventing physics, determining ground truth, deﬁning the correct answer,
or generating problem semantics that are then adopted without veriﬁcation. The cue span is stored
explicitly with each family so that artefact checks can conﬁrm members differ only within it, and
the construction-stage artefact report records 30 of 30 templates passing cue-span isolation.

These analyses reﬁne the picture in Section 5.
(a) Cue-speciﬁc probe directions. Cue A and Cue B probe weight vectors at layer 16 have cosine

and 0.511 (B to A) on the other. Together with the shared causal heads of Section 5.4, this indicates

I F AILURE TAXONOMY
The monitor’s conﬁdent errors cluster into four structural categories, which bears on how it would
be deployed.
(a) Dangerous template, clean rejection. CM_B_UM_018, an Ohm’s-law family with a unit-matched
isolated branch current, is scored 0.966 with a measured Slp θ of exactly 0. CM_B_UM_041, momen-
tum to velocity with a disconnected 6.0 m/s distractor, is scored 0.998 with Slp θ = 0 .203. Both instantiate the geometry of a sensitive family without the numerical proximity that triggers the be-
haviour.
(b) Right shell, distant distractor .CM_B_UM_035 and CM_B_UM_053, pendulum and spring-mass
families with frequency distractors at 0.5 and 0.8 Hz, are scored high and remain behaviourally
stable.
(c) Weak positives underestimated. CM_B_UM_015 (Slp θ = 0 .578, scored 0.125) and
CM_B_UM_027 (Slp θ = 0 .719, scored 0.131) clear the threshold with cue values that are not es- pecially close to the answer.
(d) Strong positives with dispersed signatures. CM_B_UM_009 (Slp θ = 4 .375, scored 0.309),
CM_B_004 (Slp θ = 2 .672, scored 0.213) and CM_A_STD_019 (Slp θ = 3 .156, scored 0.365) are unambiguous positives the layer-18 mean feature underestimates.
Categories (a) and (b) are the same error viewed at different conﬁdence levels, and they say the
probe reads template class. Category (d) says a single mean feature does not capture every route to
high sensitivity. Together they motivate the numerically aware per-rendering representation named
in Section 5.5.
J S COPE OF THE CLAIMS
All results are on open-weight models between 7B and 32B parameters, on text-only physics prob-
lems in three domains with four renderings per family, under teacher-forced scoring of a single refer-
ence answer. The parser is part of the measurement deﬁnition, and while Slp θ is parser-independent
by construction, Sﬂ θ inherits its coverage. We do not claim these ﬁndings extend to multimodal
physics, closed-weight models, or traces produced under sampling. Within these bounds the com-
parisons are internally consistent: every predictor in Figure 3 is scored on the same 135 families
under the same leave-one-family-out split, and every interval resamples the same unit.

Balance. No single surface feature predicts the label: orientation-independent AUC is 0.503 for
prompt length, 0.509 for token count, 0.557 for number count. Domain shows no association with
the label (Cramér’s V = 0.092, p = 0.551). Cue type ( V = 0.251, p = 0.012) and template cluster
(V = 0 .382, p = 0 .009) do associate, which is expected given Table 1 and is the reason every
evaluation in this paper holds out whole families. A family-row stratiﬁed classiﬁer over surface fea-
tures alone reaches 0.612 with reﬁt permutation p = 0 .015, below every sensitivity-trained probe
in Table 4 except two. These are the embedding-layer probe, which reads the raw input representa-
tion before any transformer processing, and the prompt-condition probe, which is trained on the cue
value (a Level-1 target), not on sensitivity. Fifteen families without authoritative labels are excluded.
An earlier version of this audit coerced them to negative and is superseded.
Leakage. A structure-ﬁrst audit over the full untruncated population of 620 prompts and 155 fami-
lies ﬁnds 0 cross-split exact duplicates. Of 24,354 candidate pairs after blocking, 21,072 are resolved
as shared prompt scaffold and 930 are within-family expected pairs, leaving 2,352 unresolved cross-
family rendering pairs, corresponding to 147 family pairs in 34 clusters and touching 110 families.
Active detectors are exact prompt hash, number-masked prompt hash, normalised symbolic equa-
tion hash, cue template hash, variable-renaming invariant structure hash, and numeric instantiation
sibling detection. Lexical similarity is used only as a tiebreaker, because the shared prompt scaffold
makes a lexical threshold near-universal, a defect that affected an earlier version of this audit, which
is superseded here. Semantic template similarity was unavailable without a local embedding method
and is recorded as not run. Template clusters are a coarser grouping than family identity. The pri-
mary evaluation (Section 4) holds out whole families but not whole clusters, so this audit does not
rule out a held-out family sharing cluster-level structure with a family in its training fold.
Provenance. 182 science runs carry real scheduler job identities, with registry coverage 0.703
and strict coverage 0.451 against that denominator. All critical artefacts carry validated resolution
records, and all ten tracked claims cite an authoritative artefact. Every headline number in this paper
is registered against a named artefact with an allowed and prohibited wording note, and the wording
used in Sections 5.1 through 7 conforms to those notes.
Human validation status. Dual independent validation of cue irrelevance is speciﬁed, with an an-
notation schema, qualiﬁcation test, calibration bank, validator handbook and stratiﬁed pilot selection
all committed. The pilot has not been run, and until it is, the benchmark’s cue-irrelevance claims rest
on symbolic certiﬁcation alone. Symbolic certiﬁcation is the stronger of the two guarantees for the
invariance itself, since it proves the answer cannot change. Human validation addresses the separate
question of whether a cue is plausibly irrelevant to a physicist reading the prompt, which certiﬁcation
does not establish.
