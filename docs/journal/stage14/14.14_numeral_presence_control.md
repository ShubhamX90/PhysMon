### J14.14-001 · 2026-10-18 · decision
kind: decision
**What:** Build the matched control that J14.12-002 found missing: score, under every cell prompt, each numeral that appears in the prompt as a candidate answer (target unit, `answer_line` wrapper, written exactly as in the prompt), tagged by role, together with a matched absent control (same shape, not in the prompt, not within 1% of the gold).
**Why:** the pilot showed that a same-unit cue numeral becomes a much more likely answer and that the boost depends on unit compatibility (J14.9-006). It could not show whether this is specific to an irrelevant cue or a general property of any number present in the prompt, including the numbers the problem needs. The presence effect (log-probability of a present numeral minus that of its matched absent control, under the same prompt) can be compared across roles: relevant input, isolated cue with the same unit, isolated cue with an incompatible unit, metadata, and relevant reference (the role-reversal cell).
**Design [V]:** the plan reuses the job format of the pilot planner so the existing runner can score it; the candidate string is the job's `gold_text` and the real gold is kept in `true_gold_value` (stated in `candidate_note`). Controls come from a per-numeral seeded generator (seed 42). Paired contrasts are within a prompt (cue effect minus the mean effect of the relevant inputs of the same prompt) and between the same-unit and incompatible-unit cells, which share the cue digits.
**Limits stated before the run:** matched shape does not match digit frequency, so a small presence effect is read against the dtype noise floor (0.1 to 0.3 nats) and against the spread of controls; the numerals in a prompt are not independent; ten structures.
**Tests [V]:** `test_numeral_presence_s154.py` 6 passed (numeral extraction, control shape and distance, determinism, one control per present numeral, role tags, no decoys, directory refusal); `test_numeral_presence_analysis_s155.py` 5 passed on synthetic jobs and records with known effects (presence effect, roles, paired contrasts, NOT_EVALUATED, join errors, outputs).

### J14.14-003 · 2026-10-18 · decision
kind: decision
**Frozen analysis, decided before any presence score exists** (implemented in `scripts/v2/s155_numeral_presence_analysis.py`):
1. Presence effect per numeral = log-probability of the numeral as an answer (target unit, `answer_line`) minus that of its matched absent control under the same prompt; per prompt and role the effects are averaged, then by structure; cluster bootstrap over the ten structures (2,000 resamples, seed 42).
2. Roles: `relevant_input`, `cue_nongoverning_isolated` split by unit type (same, incompatible), `cue_irrelevant_metadata`, `cue_relevant_reference`; `relevant_input` is also reported for the no-cue prompts alone.
3. Key paired contrasts, within a prompt: each cue role minus the mean effect of the relevant inputs of the same prompt; and the same-unit near cue minus the incompatible-unit near cue (same digits).
4. A numeral whose record is not OK or whose control does not exist is NOT_EVALUATED, never zero. No p-values.
5. The effects are read against the dtype noise floor (0.1 to 0.3 nats, J14.9-004), and no statement about relevance gating is made from them: they say whether the boost is specific to an isolated cue or shared by every number present in the prompt.
**Runner:** the score-only runner `scripts/v2/s158_score_only_run.py` (a new file; the pilot runner is untouched) with the template `slurm/templates/stage14_score_only.sh`; its 6 tests pass, including an independent recomputation of the stored log-probabilities.

**What this shows [V/I]:** [V] every kind of number present in a prompt is more probable as an answer than a matched absent number (all role intervals above zero), including the numbers the problem needs; so a presence boost is not specific to an isolated cue. [V] the boost is larger for a cue numeral than for a relevant input, and largest when the cue carries the requested unit; at 8 nats for Qwen the difference is far above the dtype noise floor (0.1 to 0.3 nats, J14.9-004). [I] a role-reversal cue (the numeral is now a relevant reference) still carries a boost well above that of the relevant inputs, so the boost does not track relevance to the question in this design.
**Confound that this control does not remove [I]:** the matched control is any same-shape numeral that is not in the prompt and not within 1% of the gold. A cue numeral is chosen near the gold or at a stated proximity, so it may simply be a more plausible numeric answer than a random control, independent of any copying; and a relevant input is a quantity of the same kind as other inputs, not an answer. The incompatible-unit cue (same digits, different unit) is the cleanest contrast, and it is smaller than the same-unit cue; it does not separate copying from plausibility either. No statement about relevance gating or about the mechanism is made.
