1 Status, scope, and rules of evidence
Purpose. Replace, rather than lightly revise, the June 2026 Phase-II plan with an independently rea-
soned, falsifiable, executable research programme. Revision v2.0 adds a mandatory benchmark
red-team and reconstruction programme (Phase B), after a critical audit of DECOY-v1. The
previous v1.0 research redesign is preserved downstream with revised order and conditional-
ity. The target is a scientifically strong future ICLR-calibre submission, not a guarantee of acceptance.
The programme starts from the actual submitted 25-page ICLR 2027 paper , Confidence Without
Cause: Predicting and Localizing Physics-Shortcut Sensitivity in Language Model Hidden States (submis-
sion 50608), and treats its experiments as reported Phase-I findings requiring reproduction . The
prior 30-page proposal, its 14-page supplement, the 42-page Phase-II plan, and the OpenReview/PAT
feedback are historical context, not authorities on what must be done next. The differently titled 29-
page Proved, Not Annotated file has not been established as the submitted manuscript and must not
be confused with it.
Critical epistemic constraint. A symbolic certificate that the correct physical answer is invariant to
cue edits does not prove that the model’s probability of a particular answer string ought to remain
invariant; it also does not prove cue use in the model’s reasoning. Cue effects on probabilities, in-
ternal representations, correct/incorrect answers, and physically faithful computation are separate
estimands. A first-rate study must show which estimands move and why. Throughout this protocol: S
means an observational sensitivity measure; mechanism requires controlled, specific, predictive in-
tervention evidence; shortcut reliance is reserved for evidence that model computation improperly
incorporates the irrelevant cue, not merely a log-probability fluctuation.
Research thesis to test, not presume. Some irrelevant numerical distractors compete with the task-
relevant quantity during intermediate processing, and this competition is mediated by a structured
and partially generalizable internal pathway. Distinct alternative theses are (i) language/answer-
string probability and formatting effects, (ii) generic difficulty or uncertainty, (iii) numerical copying
/ answer contamination without intervening on the physical solution, (iv) ordinary sensitivity of con-
ditional language-model distributions to context, and (v) multiple unrelated cue-specific mechanisms.
All are legitimate outcomes. Do not design experiments whose analysis only accepts the preferred
hypothesis.
Scope strategy. An upstream Phase B (B0–B7) tests whether the benchmark measures a compelling,
valid phenomenon and decides whether to rebuild, narrow, or pivot. The later mechanistic critical
path contains twelve blocks (P0–P11), some of which implement or deepen Phase-B checks rather than
starting a separate dataset enterprise. A subsequent research catalogue gives compulsory controls, sec-
ondary branches, and high-upside extensions. Some experiments are mutually exclusive alternatives
or conditional escalations; performing every possible experiment is neither mathematically achiev-
able nor scientifically optimal. Maximize discriminating evidence per unit of model compute and
human review, while retaining a documented broad universe of possibilities. No new unvalidated
experiments should be smuggled into a headline claim after seeing outcomes.
Baseline source categories. SUBMITTED = reported in the actual ICLR manuscript, REVIEW = criticism
appearing in the supplied PAT review, LITERATURE = independently checked public work linked in Ref-
erences, and PROPOSAL = a new suggested experiment, hypothesis, threshold, or resource allocation. Nu-
merical targets in this document other than clearly marked SUBMITTED values are planning choices,
not results . Repository outputs are not attached here; therefore this plan cannot verify actual raw
data, scheduler jobs, code, or version history.

3.3 B2. Decide the correct construct before choosing how many examples to generate
B2.1 Define four different hypothesis families. (H-P) Physical relevance selection: does the model
correctly exclude non-governing physical variables and include governing variables? (H-N) Numer-
ical interference: does magnitude/precision/digit identity of an irrelevant cue compete with target
magnitude selection? (H-T) Text/interface effects: does number placement or tokenization alter a sin-
gle reference-answer string without changing the physics solution? (H-U) Unit/representation trans-
formations: does the model represent equivalent governing quantities consistently? Do not pool the
fourth with the first three.
B2.2 Specify primary behavior as an interpretable contrast. For each physically matched base
task, observe deterministic answer correctness and answer class under (i) no cue, (ii) irrelevant cue of

4 The falsifiable theoretical framework
Let a physical task be T=(r,a,g,y*) , with relevant variables r, assumptions a, governing equation g,
certified answer y*, and nuisance cue z. A well-designed family contains independently varied cue
z, numerical relevant variables, wording, output wrapper , and cue-placement/representation factors
while recording what is held fixed. Y*(x) is physically invariant for certified nuisance edits. The model
has a distribution p_theta(.|x,q) depending on prompt x and answer-scoring protocol q; never omit
q when studying probabilities.
Observable endpoints (distinct by construction):
1. C_gen: free-generation correctness under an independently audited parser , with abstentions and
invalid outputs explicit.
2. A_gen: normalized parsed free-generation answer; F_flip: pairwise answer disagreement within
family, stratified by correctness transitions.
3. L_gold(x,q): length-normalized and raw teacher-forced log-probability assigned to a specified
canonical answer sequence under protocol q.
4. D_lp: distribution of within-family gold-answer likelihoods; primary reference-free candidate
max_i L_gold - min_i L_gold , accompanied by SD/MAD and pairwise absolute differences. Unlike
the legacy signed score, this is invariant to variant ordering, but it loses the distinction between
decline and increase; report signed contrasts separately against a semantically defined no-cue
anchor .

5. P_gold: restricted answer-set normalized probability when a defensible set of answer alterna-
tives exists; compare with first-answer-token and answer-token-chain scores, fully acknowl-
edging closed-set conditioning.
6. R_alt: log-odds log p(y* | x,q) - log p(y_decoy | x,q) for independently prespecified distractor-

9. I_select: a selective intervention’s effect on nuisance sensitivity minus its effect on genuinely
relevant information, answer accuracy and unrelated tasks.
Primary scientific distinction. An ideal symbolic solver yields the same answer value across irrele-
vant edits. It need not be modeled as assigning exactly equal probability to a fixed byte string under
differently worded contexts. Probability invariance is a desirable robustness convention , not en-
tailed by the mathematical certificate. The new study must test whether different notions of instability
cohere empirically, rather than treating one as a theorem-induced truth.

4.2 Claim hierarchy and evidence gates
• C1 (benchmark): certified task-answer invariance plus audited natural-language fidelity, inde-
pendence, coverage, and well-specified exclusions.
• C2 (behavioral): reproducible cue-specific behavioral instability on unseen template clusters,
reported across multiple endpoints rather than one answer-string likelihood.
• C3 (monitor): correctly scoped single-input monitoring with meaningful paired incremental
value beyond tuned available baselines and enough precision to resolve deployment tradeoffs.
• C4 (causal localization): multiple valid intervention families identify specific components,
replicated on a held-out causal panel and not explained by model damage.
• C5 (mechanistic explanation): a concrete, falsifiable functional account with necessity + con-
trolled sufficiency + upstream/downstream path tests + causal abstraction/generalization.
• C6 (repair): selective, generalizing intervention outperforming appropriate nonmechanistic al-
ternatives with a documented utility/cost frontier .
Promotion rule. A claim only advances one level when the corresponding tests pass; a highly signif-
icant C3 monitor does not establish C4/C5. The next paper needs either a convincing C5, or an excep-
tionally well-validated C1–C3 methodological finding with true novelty beyond current benchmark
literature, preferably C6 if feasible.

5.8 P7. Identify what the causal components actually compute
Question. What computable function connects distractor tokens to altered answer tendencies? Lo-
calization without function is insufficient for a high-impact mechanism claim.
P7.1 Anatomize head 11 at L16. For every token position (governing variables, cue value, cue unit,
isolation/irrelevance phrase, problem question, last prompt position, assistant prefix), measure QK
attention patterns, attention logits, output vector norm, OV map contribution and direct residual/logit
attribution across controlled factorial pairs. Do not treat an attention heatmap as causal evidence.
Patch attention probabilities and value vectors separately; patch source-token contributions into the
target position, then test whether they mediate the response. Compare input-token-level Q/K/V sites
and output-projection pathways with attention/MLP processing nearby.

5.9 P8. Recover and validate upstream/downstream computation, not just one head
P8.1 Define sources and sinks before attribution. Sources: cue numeric tokens, cue unit token, se-
mantic isolation phrase, physically relevant quantities, governing-law words, output-format instruc-
tion. Candidate sinks: L16 H11 (or discovered stable mediator), L18 diagnostic state, correct-vs-decoy
answer logit. Predefine the desired causal contrast: near-match irrelevant cue vs far-match irrele-
vant cue with shared target answer; add a separate relevant-edit contrast in which the gold answer
legitimately changes.
P8.2 Two independent discovery procedures. Use gradient-based edge attribution/attribution
patching (and integrated-gradient refinements when attribution saturates) as a candidate ranking
method, plus targeted clean/corrupt activation patching at layer/position/head/MLP sites. Optionally
compare circuit mask optimization. Causal confirmation uses true interventions, not approximate
gradients; attribute graph methods can be misleading under reconstruction errors and feature
approximation.
P8.3 Path patching. Rank upstream attention heads, MLP outputs and input-token contributions to
the mediator . Apply receiver-conditioned path patching so a candidate source is changed while alter-
native branches are held fixed as far as the implementation permits. Repeat for source →mediator ,
mediator→downstream layer , and downstream→answer . Check token and hook order , causal graph
direction, numerical tolerance and reverse interventions. Demonstrate that the identified path mat-
ters only when the mediator receives the source information.
P8.4 Knockout–rescue logic. (a) Knock out upstream source, measure lost effect; (b) rescue with
the mediator’s clean activation, test restoration; (c) knock out mediator , attempt upstream rescue and
predict failure if mediator is necessary; (d) control with unrelated and matched same-answer donors;
(e) rescue a role-reversal relevant-edit task to show the path is not simply a destructive filter . Contrast
against rescuing a generic next-token loss change. This sequence is a stringent causal ordering test.
P8.5 Circuit completeness and minimality. For a hypothesized edge set, measure faithfulness:
run the model retaining only hypothesized pathways versus replacing complementary pathways with
matched control activations. Evaluate sufficiency, comprehensiveness, selectivity, stability across cor-
ruption choices and path sparsity. Generate faithfulness-vs-circuit-size curves and a specificity-vs-size
frontier; report residual unexplained effect. Check that random matched graphs and a trivial high-
capacity whole-residual patch do not provide the same explanatory power per component.
P8.6 Causal abstraction validation. Write an explicit high-level algorithm (e.g., parse relevant vari-
ables → parse candidate numeric values → evaluate relevance/unit consistency → choose candidate
answer); define which neural subspaces map to each high-level variable. Use interchange interven-
tions on paired input families and test whether the low-level model’s responses match interventions
predicted by the high-level algorithm. Vary one high-level variable at a time while holding correlated
variables matched. Poor interchange agreement falsifies the proposed explanation even if component
knockouts are large.
P8.7 Representational bottleneck hypotheses. Test whether the mediator stores raw value, nor-
malized magnitude, a relevance flag, a source identifier , or a merged answer candidate. Probe sepa-
rate source/value/role subspaces, conduct orthogonal projection ablations and donor swaps, quantify
whether the same component acts as a routing gate or downstream logit amplifier . Confirm on entirely
held-out laws, magnitudes, prompts and cue positions.
P8.8 Sparse features: optional, validation-heavy. If the low-rank/head-level account stalls, com-
pare existing open SAEs, transcoders/cross-layer transcoders, supervised distributed alignment search
and ordinary MLP neurons on equal compute and feature budgets . Evaluate reconstruction error ,
feature stability under seeds, causal interchange and suppression specificity; do not equate a seman-
tically appealing SAE feature with a mechanism. MIB reports that SAEs are not always better than
neuron baselines for causal variable localization; treat SAEs as a method to test , not a badge of depth.

5.10 P9. Cross‐model, cross‐domain and post‐training generalization
P9.1 Cross-architecture confirmation. Freeze the function hypothesis, not the head index. Use fresh
discovery samples to localize functionally analogous components in a second architecture; evaluate
the hypothesis on untouched clusters with matched causal endpoints. Distinguish transfer of a causal
variable/function from transfer of individual hidden-state directions or identical layer depths. A
functional analog in a different head is replication; a naive matched-layer knockout failing is not a
complete null.
P9.2 Scaling ladder .Conditional on compute, evaluate small→medium→large checkpoints within a
model family, avoiding a giant number of models that each receive superficial analysis. The core com-
parison should control architecture and training stage enough to interpret causal differences. Report
behavior across scale before choosing expensive causal cases; instrument only models with stable
access to required hooks and answer/trace scoring.
P9.3 Same-backbone post-training. Compare base/instruct and, where available, same-backbone
reasoning fine-tunes, with changes to system and answer prompting controlled. Analyze whether
post-training changes rate of error , confidence sensitivity, identified causal function and component al-
location. Do not attribute differences to RL without isolating comparable training runs or stating that
multiple post-training changes are confounded.
P9.4 Familiar-to-novel-law transfer .Create new physically valid symbolic worlds or dimensionally
coherent toy laws with unfamiliar names/variables, teaching necessary rules explicitly. If a numeric
shortcut generalizes to these tasks, it suggests a broader numerical/linguistic mechanism; if it disap-
pears, familiar physics priors may be essential. Make sure the toy laws are learnable and that difficulty
is matched by solver-assisted pilots.
P9.5 Source and language transfer . Test human-written problems, new problem formats,
terse structured equations, narrative descriptions and controlled bilingual renderings where na-
tive/qualified validators can certify semantic equivalence. Record tokenization differences rather
than confounding translation with mechanism. English-only results do not imply cross-lingual
generality.
P9.6 Cue composition and adversarial boundary. Add 2–3 simultaneously irrelevant cues; vary
distractor congruence/contradiction, near-match competition and ordering. Look for additive, super-
additive or suppressive interaction in behavior and circuit effect. A mechanism that only explains
single numerical cues must be scoped accordingly. Adversarially search for maximally sensitive valid
held-out examples under a fixed query budget, then retest on freshly authored confirmation examples
to control optimizer overfitting.
P9.7 Persistence across tools and precision. Use two independently implemented hook backends
when feasible; compare fp16/bf16 and unquantized vs modest quantization (with a numerical
agreement gate). If a component’s effect depends on a software implementation bug, retire it.
Cross-backend reproducibility is a stronger assurance than adding unrelated models.

5.11 P10. Mechanism‐guided repair and comparative efficacy
P10.1 Define operational loss. Optimize weighted free-generation error , cue-related answer-flips,
correct/decoy log-odds, calibration and OOD robustness, subject to maximum allowable degradation
on no-cue and relevant-variable tasks. Avoid optimizing only a teacher-forced gold-likelihood statistic,
which could be improved by reducing likelihood everywhere or artificially flattening logits.
P10.2 Nonmechanistic baseline ladder . Remove the distractor with a deterministic semantic filter;
prompt an explicit ‘ignore irrelevant information’ instruction; rephrase into governing variables; an-
swer via a small symbolic solver; use two-query consistency checking; majority vote/self-consistency;
targeted counterfactual augmentation/fine-tuning. Compare latency/compute and the damage from
mistakenly ignoring relevant variables. This ladder sets the bar for any purportedly superior circuit
edit.
P10.3 Training-free circuit interventions. Head-level mean/no-cue replacement conditioned on a
high-specificity trigger; linearly project out a causal cue-direction; gate a proven source→mediator
edge; apply tiny direction subtraction/addition at a specified site; selectively patch from a matched
no-cue rendering. Tune intensity on development only, with a strength-vs-damage curve. Detect un-
intended damage on prompts where the same numeral becomes relevant.
P10.4 Monitoring-triggered repair . First measure the actual single-prompt monitor’s precision and
FPR under shift; test selective edit only for the triggered subset and compare with applying the same
edit to everyone. Show deployment benefit under realistic base rates and decision costs. An oracle
trigger based on four future variants is only a nondeployable upper bound.
P10.5 Training-based mitigation. If justified, train LoRA or lightweight fine-tunes using certificate-
backed paired counterfactual consistency, plus a standard answer-correctness loss and relevance-
preservation controls. Contrast with random distractor augmentation, strong-distractor curricula
(cf. GSM-DC) and generic instruction tuning. Keep entire governing-law clusters out of training and
compare relevant-variable responsiveness to avoid teaching the model to ignore all extra numbers.
Follow training to see whether the original head mechanism weakens, relocates, or is bypassed.
P10.6 Stress test attack/defense equilibrium. Optimize valid cue substitutions, wording and place-
ments to evade the monitor or defeat the intervention under a declared budget; report adaptation
curves and fresh-attack transfer . Include adaptive attacker access variants (knowledge of threshold
only, monitor score, full gradient) and certification of each generated edit. If the intervention only
fixes in-distribution examples, classify it as narrow mechanistic control.
P10.7 Repair claims require causal linkage. Compare targeted edit, equal-norm random edit,
nearby-layer edit, alternative-component edit, prompt-only baseline, and unedited. If repair
improves outcomes but the learned mechanism does not explain why, report a useful empirical
intervention without implying a causal circuit. A strong claim connects pathway suppression to
counterfactual behavioral improvement and rescue/relapse.
Gate P10. A positive result requires held-out paired improvement in a practically meaningful out-
come (preferably correctness/answer competition), statistically resolved harm bounds, and advantage
or distinctive tradeoff against simple nonmechanistic methods. If basic cue removal works better and
with less damage, do not pretend an expensive neural edit is operationally superior; it may still be a
valuable mechanistic test.

5.12 P11. Confirmatory closure, adversarial review and publishable claim freeze
P11.1 Build the paper around one causal chain, not the chronological lab log. Figure 1:
invariant-task causal factorial and hypotheses. Figure 2: measurement audit separating gold-string,
restricted-choice and generated answers. Figure 3: cue-feature interaction on independent clusters.
Figure 4: true patch/knockout/rescue and absolute effect sizes. Figure 5: upstream path with
counterfactual predictions. Figure 6: replication and/or selective repair . Include failures in compact
explicit tables, not buried in supplements.
P11.2 Mandatory final audit. Independent recomputation of main numbers, validation signatures,
code and model versions, held-out contamination audit, pre-registration deviations, multiple-testing
ledger , negative and zero-row results, exact intervention sites, confidence intervals, relevant base-
lines, alternative metrics, artifacts and statistical unit. Ask an unaffiliated research peer to attack the
strongest claim with the raw evidence and with only the released protocol. Attempt to falsify before
submission.
P11.3 Two-stage external confirmation. Internal hold-out is used for one explicit confirmatory de-
cision. A fully untouched external cohort from newly written/law-held-out materials is opened once
after hypotheses, functional representation, causal site and inference code are frozen. All additional
tuning after opening the external cohort converts it into development; obtain new data for a subse-
quent confirmation.
P11.4 Reproducible release. Include rendered prompts, symbolic programs, typed equations, certifi-
cates, validator labels/adjudications, all eligible model outputs and log probabilities, inclusion meta-
data, per-family out-of-fold predictions, raw intervention before/after endpoints, code, environment,
random seeds, hardware profiles and manifest checksums. Publish both passing and failing runs with
an explicit authority hierarchy; include anonymized artifacts while under double-blind review where
permitted.
P11.5 Make venue choice contingent on the result. An ICLR-level mechanism narrative needs
a substantial new insight , not the most pages of supplemental ablations. If the core contribution
becomes measurement science, consider an evaluation/benchmark framing. If the central advance is
trained mitigation, frame the paper as robustness/causal control. Do not keep all three narratives at
equal weight unless their evidence makes a coherent, noninflated causal chain.
Gate P11. No paper submission until an independent reader can reproduce the primary figures and
state, in one paragraph, the causal explanation and the best attempt to falsify it. Disconfirming evi-
dence must be integrated into the headline scope, not relegated to future work.

8 Statistical design: standards for inference, not decoration
8.1 Independent experimental unit, estimands and non‐independence
The independent unit for a primary generalization claim is the independently authored symbolic
structure/template cluster, not a rendered prompt, paraphrase, numeric instantiation, or decoder sam-
ple. A single structure may yield hundreds of observations; counting them as hundreds of independent
examples is pseudoreplication. Model and cue variants are repeated measures, nested under clusters.
For a generalization claim to new physics laws, treat the law/program cluster as the sampling unit or
report that its population is fixed and inference is conditional on these laws. In a leave-one-domain-
out test, only a few physics domains exist; do not report ordinary asymptotic p-values as if there were
hundreds of independent domains.
Primary behavioral estimand (to finalize after P1 discovery): conditional average cue effect on
R_alt (when defined) and/or correctness transition for a randomized, certified nuisance edit, aver-
aged over a specified target distribution of physics structures and cue values. Report both population-
average and cue-stratified effects. Primary causal estimand: on a specified panel, the average of
Y(intervention) - Y(control) under a fixed, documented site and recipient definition; this is an in-
tervention on model activations, not necessarily the natural indirect effect of cue z because interven-
ing changes the computation’s ordinary distribution. Primary monitoring estimand: out-of-sample
paired difference in proper score (log loss or Brier) with AUROC as a ranking metric, conditional on
the exact information/query budget.
Report both unconditional performance across the defined population and conditional perfor-
mance on model-positive/sensitive cases. The second can be useful to understand a mechanism but is
selected on the outcome; its result cannot establish that the effect generalizes to all physics questions.
Record eligibility pre-intervention and measure no-cue/relevant control performance.
8.2 Multiple comparisons and research degrees of freedom
• Freeze a small family of confirmatory hypotheses: (H-B) behavioral factorial effect, (H-M) single-
prompt incremental monitoring value, (H-C) causal head/path effect, and optionally (H-R) selec-
tive repair . Every hypothesis maps to a specific endpoint, model, held-out cohort, effect-direction
prediction and null.
• Use hierarchical testing or Holm correction for the small primary family; use Benjamini–
Hochberg FDR for prespecified secondary scans and annotate exploratory plots without
pretending FDR correction makes them confirmatory.
• Selection over layers, heads, sites, formulations, decoders, metrics, threshold and cue conditions
is a large multi-search. Handle it by discovery selection on one set and evaluation once on
an independent set , not by announcing the best of many tests with a naïve CI.

8.3 Power and precision protocol
Before collecting confirmation labels , simulate from pilot-estimated family-cluster variance and
cue-specific distributions. Sweep realistic numbers of independent structures, 2–8 cue conditions, 2–3
numeric instantiations and models. Compute estimated CI width, type-I error with the chosen resam-
pling unit and power for minimum scientifically important contrasts. Run the exact proposed analysis
on simulated null and effect datasets; ensure false-positive rate is calibrated. Include a scenario with
20–40% loss from validation, parse failures, and ineligible causal recipients. An analysis that is too
underpowered for ±0.03 AUROC improvement must not make a ‘statistically equivalent’ claim; use an
equivalence test only after specifying a minimum practically important margin and achieving suffi-
cient power .
Sample size is decision-dependent. Starting target of 300–600 new symbolic structures is a planning
allocation for independent validity and power , not a claim that this number will resolve all compar-
isons. A tiny difference in paired AUROC may require much more than 600 if cluster correlation and
label prevalence are unfavorable. Conversely, a large, specific causal effect could require fewer un-
touched structures if all other controls are convincing. Publish the simulation and its assumptions
alongside the final chosen number .
8.4 Negative and missing result policy
A zero-row job, failed hook, timeout, nonparseable response, ineligible family, or untested control is
NOT_EVALUATED, not a null effect. Keep a missingness codebook with reason, first_seen, planned_run,
attempts, eligible_population, and intended analysis. Report exclusions and completeness both by
cue and by model. If a test fails after looking at response scores, keep all original units in a sensitivity
analysis, including worst-case bounds for missing outcomes. Never silently replace data-dependent
missing values with zero or negative labels.
9 Compute, human effort, storage and reproducibility architecture
Hardware assumption to verify. The June proposal described access patterns of up to 2×H200,
3×H100, 2×A100 , not a guaranteed simultaneous dedicated allocation. Before beginning, check cur-
rently available scheduler partitions, actual per-job GPU limits, software stacks and wall-time quota.
Specific throughput and costs must be measured on Sharanga rather than invented here. Models
should be reselected against present availability, licensing and stable hook support; avoid commit-
ting to a 397B-class model merely because an old proposal mentioned one.
Cost model. For behavioral runs, estimate N_structures × N_numeric × N_cue_cells × N_models ×
(N_score_forward + N_generated_resamples) and record separately the repeated output-format wrap-
pers. For activation jobs, additionally multiply by selected layer/site/head/position sets and donor-
recipient pairs; cache only what is actually reused. One full all-layer/all-token dump can grow to
terabytes for large models; the submitted manuscript cites a 1.57 TB upper-bound design motivat-
ing staged extraction. Recompute memory from n_items × n_positions × n_layers × d_model ×
bytes_per_value for current models rather than copying this figure.

11.1 What could actually be novel (conditional claims)
N1: A validated, reference-independent measurement of physics-specific nuisance influence that
jointly captures gold-answer probability, distractor-answer competition and generated-answer be-
havior , with explicit separation of language-model scoring artifacts.Risk: GSM-DC and GSM-Symbolic
already offer strong controlled benchmarks; a new metric alone may not suffice.
N2: A mechanistically predictive function for irrelevant numerical information (e.g., a quantified
relevance-gating or numeral-copy pathway) established by orthogonal cue factorials, knockout, recip-
rocal transplantation, path patching, rescue and causal abstraction on unseen physics computation
graphs. Risk: broader class-based misgeneralization and arithmetic heuristics are already known;
the specific functional discovery must exceed them.
N3: A rigorous demonstration that a single-prompt internal monitor adds (or does not add) useful
information beyond properly nested fitted uncertainty, correctness and numerical-template baselines,
with independent cluster/source transfer . Risk: a small unresolvable AUC difference, by itself, is not
a major contribution.

rectness/reliability under valid nuisance edits without disabling legitimate use of relevant variables,

15 Definition of scientific completion
A final ICLR-calibre submission is scientifically ready only after Phase B passes its applicable gate ,
and only when it can answer all of the following, with independently verifiable artifacts: (1) What
precise model behavior changed, under which definition, and why should the physical answer be
invariant? Which separate tests independently establish natural-language physical validity
and matching of the encoded graph? (2) What weaker scoring/format/difficulty hypothesis was
strongest, and where was it falsified? (3) Did a monitor add genuine information beyond fair inexpen-
sive baselines on unseen structures, and if not is this acknowledged?(4) What neural computation was
identified beyond an effect of removing a head? (5) Which controlled intervention predictions would
fail if the proposed mechanistic story were false, and what happened when tested? (6) Which models,
cue types, structures and prompting conditions do and do not replicate? (7) Can intervention improve
a meaningful outcome without disrupting legitimately relevant numerical information? (8) Can an
independent researcher reproduce both successes and null results, including denominators and exclu-
sions? (9) Is the core insight substantially different from GSM-DC, GSM-Symbolic, numerical-heuristic
circuits and existing causal interpretability work? (10) Does the manuscript say no more than the
evidence supports?
If the answer to (4) or (5) is no, the project can still produce rigorous measurement science, but it
should not market itself as a completed mechanistic explanation. If (1) fails, return to the measure-
ment definition regardless of how impressive other metrics look.
